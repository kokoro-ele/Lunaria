Guides / Birthday Moon

Personal astronomy · Four simple steps

What was the Moon phase on your birthday?

Enter your birth date, local time, and birthplace in Lunaria to reconstruct the calculated Moon phase and how the lunar disk was oriented in your local sky.

You do not need an exact birth time to identify the general phase. The Moon's illumination changes gradually. Time and place matter more when you want its altitude, direction, horizon status, and precise visual orientation.

Find your birthday Moon

How to look it up

  1. Choose your birth date. Use the date that was recorded locally where you were born.
  2. Enter the local clock time. If you do not know it, use 12:00 noon. The phase will still be representative, but the sky position may not be.
  3. Select your birthplace. Click the globe near the city or region. Lunaria determines the time zone from those coordinates and applies historical daylight-saving rules available in the browser.
  4. Read, save, and share. Review phase, illumination, moon age, distance, and local position. Create a keepsake image or copy a link that restores the same settings.

What the result means

Phase
A familiar name such as waxing crescent, first quarter, full moon, or waning gibbous.
Illumination
The percentage of the Earth-facing lunar disk lit by the Sun at that moment.
Moon age
The elapsed time since the preceding astronomical new moon.
Local view
Whether it was above the horizon, where it was in the sky, and how its bright limb was rotated.

Common questions

Do I need my exact birth time?

No. The date is sufficient for a useful phase and illumination estimate. Use noon when the time is unknown, and treat altitude and direction as illustrative rather than biographical facts.

Can people born on the same date see different results?

Their lunar phase will generally be very similar. A different time or place can change the local calendar date, horizon status, compass direction, and apparent rotation of the Moon.

Is a birthday Moon an astrology reading?

No. Lunaria reports astronomical quantities calculated from the Sun, Earth, Moon, time, and observer location. It does not assign personality traits or make predictions.
