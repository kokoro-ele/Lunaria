What changes with location?

The phase and illuminated fraction are almost the same everywhere at one instant. Your location does change where the Moon sits relative to the horizon, the direction you face to see it, and how the lunar disk is rotated relative to “up.” That is why photographs taken at the same moment from different hemispheres can look rotated, even though they show the same phase.

Transparent calculations

Lunaria uses the open-source Astronomy Engine library for ephemeris calculations, topocentric positions, lunar illumination, distance, and libration. Coordinates are mapped to an IANA time zone so a selected wall-clock time can be interpreted with the relevant daylight-saving rule.

The visualization is intended for education and personal keepsakes. It does not model terrain, buildings, clouds, atmospheric transparency, or daylight brightness and should not replace professional navigation or observatory software.
